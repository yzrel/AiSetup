The expected outputs were partially realized, especially during the peak production period. However, the sustainability of these outcomes was undermined by a confluence of external economic variables.
The company experienced decline in market demand for both its primary coconut husk products and its bukopie line. This downturn in sales directly contributed to theclosure of its physical storewhich had become financially unsustainable. The combined impact of reduced consumer interest and rising operational costsparticularly rental fees and overhead expensesfurther strained the company's resources. Due to the increased cost of raw materials, utilities, and logistics brought by inflation, the owner cannot maintain the day-to-day operations and sustain the overall business viability.

The company is currently in Phase II of its SETUP implementation as they successfully completed Phase I in 2016. During the initial phase, the firm underwent key technical interventions, including the conduct of Manufacturing Productivity Extension (MPEX) Program and an Energy Audit.
VIII. Problems met, and action taken during project implementation
Minor constraints were encountered during project implementationparticularly during the procurement stagedue to the cooperator's decision to modify the originally approved equipment. However, these adjustments were manageable and were promptly addressed through proper coordination and adherence to procedural requirements with objectives remained on track.
IX. Status of Funds and Equipment Acquired thru the Project
The project fund has been fully disbursedand all procured equipment, as listed above, was verified to be present and accounted for during the project monitoring visit conducted in coordination with RPMO.
X. Reason for Termination/Withdrawal
The company has submitted a formal letter of intent to voluntarily return selected equipment citing financial constraints because of their inability to sustain payment obligations. The primary reason that contributed to this decision was the closure of their physical store and the discontinuation of production activities. The request for pullout was initiated solely at the discretion of the cooperatorbased on their current challenges.

XII. Impact of Intervention (if applicable)
The intervention initially resulted in enhanced productivity, improved product quality, and more streamlined process through acquisition of appropriate equipment, and adherence to food safety standards. However, the business later faced financial constraints, and the closure of its physical storeaffected the sustained utilization of the acquired technologies.
XIII. Final Recommendation
Proceed with the pull-out of all equipment not currently in use by the cooperator and still in good operational condition. The final obligation shall be recomputed with consideration of depreciation.  A revised refund schedule shall then be prepared based on the adjusted values.
